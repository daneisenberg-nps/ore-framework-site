Foundations
===========

Operational Resilience Engineering builds on, and seeks to contribute to,
several established bodies of research. It does not use the term resilience as
a single settled concept.

Resilience engineering
----------------------


A central foundation is the work of David D. Woods on resilience engineering.
Woods distinguishes four important concepts that are often grouped under the
single label *resilience* :cite:p:`woods2015`:

* **Rebound:** returning toward an earlier condition after trauma or disruption.
* **Robustness:** continuing to function despite disturbance.
* **Graceful extensibility:** extending performance or adaptive capacity when
  surprise challenges established boundaries.
* **Sustained adaptability:** maintaining the ability to adapt as conditions,
  demands, stakeholders, and constraints continue to change.

ORE adopts these distinctions as a starting point while developing a formal
language for relating them to system states, performance boundaries,
feasibility, intervention choices, and decision perspectives.

Graceful extensibility and brittleness
--------------------------------------

Woods characterizes graceful extensibility as the opposite of brittleness
:cite:p:`woods2015,woods2018`. A brittle system can appear successful within familiar conditions but may
collapse abruptly when demands or disturbances exceed its capacity to respond.
Graceful extensibility concerns the capacity to stretch or bring additional
adaptive resources to bear when those boundaries are approached.

This distinction is especially relevant to critical infrastructure systems,
where reserve capacity, backup resources, coordination, and changes in
operational strategy can determine whether a disruption becomes a sudden loss
of service or a manageable change in operating mode.

Operations research
-------------------

ORE also draws on operations research to make explicit the decision structures
that are sometimes implicit in resilience discussions. These include decision
and design spaces, constraints, feasibility, performance measures, uncertainty,
and the relation between alternative interventions and operational outcomes.

ORE's contribution
------------------

The intended contribution of ORE is not to replace established resilience
engineering concepts. It is to develop a formal framework in which
vulnerability, resilience, operational boundaries, feasible interventions, and
interpretive perspectives can be specified and related.
